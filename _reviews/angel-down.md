Five bedraggled and downtrodden soldiers come upon a fallen angel in No Man's Land during WWI. These are not your typical heroes: they're mean, foul-mouthed, cowardly, and the only thing they share is a mutually held fear of the "Jerries" shooting at them.

One of the best reviews of this book said something like, "This book is for anyone who thought *All Quiet on the Western Front* was too warm and fuzzy." Perfectly accurate. *Angel Down* felt like a bizarre weird-fiction journey into the mind of a shell-shocked soldier, previously a swindler and son of a preacher.

This book was mesmerizing, visceral, disgusting, hellish, and I couldn't stop listening. The audiobook narrator's rate of breathing - which must surely be a special talent because I swear he did not breathe but once a chapter - increased my blood pressure and made me feel like I needed to take a swig of battery acid, bite a leather strap, and smoke two cigarettes at once. I will need time to recover from his narration, but it was perfect for the book.

The half-star I took off is for something that might be unfair: to me, Kraus was almost perfect but needed one more pass of editing to take the language (which was truly superb) from sometimes-jumbled to cutting, flourishing, and precise. I would like to do data analysis on how many chapters, paragraphs, sentences started with "and"... And and and and. I think I may have hated this had I read it versus listened to it.

But I don't know - maybe this was actually a perfect book and I'm looking for things to ding.
