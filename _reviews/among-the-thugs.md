This book was so much more and so much better than what I expected. I'm not even interested in football, or soccer, or sport of any kind, but this book was actually hardly about any of that. It was a wild exploration of crowd violence and nationalism, through the lens of English football hooliganism in the 1980s. I was surprised by the relevance all these years later. The timeline of the book was a bit hard to follow, but maybe this is what Buford intended: the violence becomes so routine that it's hard to even pin a violent event down or differentiate it from any other weekend news story.
