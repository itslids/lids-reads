I'm not sure how to describe this. The two halves of the book, separated by many years, are relatively disjointed.

The first half is quite brutal: the story of a young Bedouin girl captured by an Israeli army unit in the Negev desert in 1949. The girl is brutally raped, shot, and buried.

The second half is about a Palestinian woman living in occupied territory, trying to find "the truth" about what happened to the girl all those years before, which she read about tangentially in a newspaper article.

The portrayal of life in an occupied territory was incredibly interesting, as was the difficulty of reconstructing history in that environment, where history is still in the making and the "victors" and occupying forces have real, visceral control over what gets recorded and saved.

But overall, the first half was difficult to read, and the two halves were too disjointed for me to say I enjoyed it.
