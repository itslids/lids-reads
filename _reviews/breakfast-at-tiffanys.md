This collection was perfection. The novella, *Breakfast at Tiffany's*, was just beyond words in its literary perfection. I love that Norman Mailer, in calling Truman Capote "the most perfect writer of [his] generation," said that he wouldn't change two words in *Breakfast at Tiffany's*.

While I love Audrey Hepburn's portrayal of Holly Golightly, the novella does the character much better justice by making her more selfish, more flawed, and less charming, which is actually kind of a huge and subversive act in 1958.

Aside from the novella, this collection also included three short stories by Capote. My favorite was the autobiographical "A Christmas Memory," about a young boy and his older, eccentric, sweet and childlike cousin, and the Christmases they spend together making fruitcake. I loved the line, "Oh my, it's fruitcake weather!" And I will be stealing that for sure.

Overall, this collection was wonderful and made me appreciate Truman Capote for more than just his incredible work in *In Cold Blood*.
